# SIGNAL — Marcus: Backstory & Psychological Vulnerability

*Companion to `signal_story_bible.md`. Marcus's personal history and the way the void picks
at it. This is a **texture layer** — it colors the occasional whisper and found note, it is
not the spine of the game. The campaign is a survival-horror escape first
(`campaign_structure.md`); this just gives a few moments somewhere personal to land.*

---

## The Incident — Age 18

Marcus gets into a fight with his father. Words are said. Frustration peaks. Marcus
shoves his dad — one moment of losing control, one act of anger in a second.

His father falls. Hits the fireplace. Cracks his head. Brain injury.

What follows:

- His mother drains the family savings for his father's medical care.
- His older sibling loses their scholarship opportunity to help support the family.
- Marcus is essentially told: get a job or get out. So he does both.

He takes the Polaris job because it pays well and it's far away. He disappears to avoid
watching what he destroyed every day.

---

## The Wound — What Marcus Believes About Himself

Marcus has spent years telling himself a story: **"I'm dangerous. I hurt people. The best
thing I can do is disappear and not matter."**

He's not running from consequences (though there are those). He's running from the belief
that he's fundamentally capable of breaking people. One moment of anger, one shove, and a
whole family's life derailed. If he stays close, he'll just do it again.

So he becomes invisible. Quiet. Forgettable. A janitor at night who doesn't interact,
doesn't get close, doesn't matter enough to hurt anyone.

**The deeper layer:** he believes *helping people is dangerous.* Because every time he's
tried to do the right thing, he's messed it up. And he's given up believing he can change that.

---

## The Void's Psychology — Methodology, Not Hunger

The void is not sophisticated. It doesn't *want* Marcus's guilt or feed on it. But it's
pragmatic. It has learned what breaks people fastest.

Bullets and pipes don't work on Marcus. So it shifts tactics: **psychological leverage.**

It doesn't need a full dark-place spiral or elaborate illusions. It uses:

- **The earbud.** A voice in his head, whispering things it shouldn't know. Things that are true.
- **Found notes.** Documents at Polaris that reference his family by name. His father. His
  sibling. How does a void infection know these names?
- **Ambient whispers.** Fragments only he'd know. Conversations with his dad that night.
  Details no stranger could know.
- **Ambiguity.** Is this real? Is the void showing him something, or is he going insane?
  The uncertainty is the weapon.

### What It Says

- "Your father still suffers because of you."
- "Your sister never forgave you."
- "You're a danger. You always break things."
- "You'll hurt anyone who trusts you. You know you will."

Not lies exactly — truths wrapped in dread. Or close enough that Marcus can't tell the
difference.

### The Knife Twist

If Marcus encounters a survivor — someone he has to help, someone counting on him — the
void escalates:

> "You'll just hurt them too. You always do. Let them go. Let them die. It's what you're
> good at."

It turns his instinct to help into his deepest fear. Because helping people is the one
thing that reliably goes wrong in Marcus's life.

---

## Why This Works

One shove, one fireplace, one family broken. That **specificity** is the power. It's not
generic trauma — it's his failure, direct and personal.

And the void doesn't need to be theatrical. A voice in the dark. Notes that shouldn't
exist. The earbud that saved him now betraying him with whispers. That's enough.

The title — **Signal** — takes on another meaning: the signal of the void, the signal of
the earbud, and the signal Marcus has been sending his whole life: *"Don't get close.
I'll hurt you."*

---

*Keep the dosage low. A handful of whispers and one or two notes across the campaign — not
a running monologue. The point is that it lands hard the few times it shows up.*
