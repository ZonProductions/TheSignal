# SIGNAL — Story Bible & Discussion Doc

*Working draft — story pivot v1. Solo dev, Unreal Engine 5, targeting 2026 production value with curated asset pipeline.*

---

## Logline

A night-shift janitor at the world's foremost scientific campus survives a containment breach that corrupts the minds of nearly everyone around him — and has to fight his way out through a facility being consumed by a primordial, mindless infection.

---

## Synopsis

Marcus, an eighteen-year-old running from the fallout of a single violent moment that shattered his family, takes a night-shift janitor job at Polaris — a remote Pacific Northwest science campus secretly containing a rift to another dimension. When the rift breaches at 2 AM, a primordial void infection pours through, corrupting the minds of nearly everyone on campus, while Marcus survives only because he was shielded inside a protected research room. Roped into a desperate containment effort by a voice guiding him from afar, he fights his way through the campus armed with little more than a pipe and an earbud that tracks the void's interference. But the void learns that brute force can't stop him — so it turns to what can, using whispers, impossible notes, and his own buried guilt to convince him that the most dangerous thing he can do is try to save anyone at all.

---

## Setting

- **The org — Polaris:** the epicenter of scientific research in the world. Prestigious, well-funded, secretive in the way all elite research orgs are without being cartoonishly evil. **Polaris knew.** They were studying and *containing* the rift — the shielded room and the breach protocols exist precisely because they knew this was possible.
- **The campus:** Remotely located in a Pacific Northwest forest. Self-contained — not just labs, but a small *community*. Employees and their families live here. Housing, amenities, infrastructure. This matters: when it falls, real people fall with it, not just faceless staff.
- **Time:** 2:00 AM. Night shift. The skeleton-crew hour. Most of the campus is asleep or winding down, which makes the breach hit a population that's unprepared and scattered.

The self-contained-community angle is one of the strongest assets here. It raises the stakes (families, homes, a whole living place) and it gives you environmental variety with a built-in logic — you're not just running through generic "labs," you're moving through a place people actually lived.

## Main Character

- **Name:** Marcus (leaning) / Isaac (alt). TBD.
- **Role:** Janitor. Working class, invisible to the science elite around him, knows the building's guts better than the people who run it. Good instinct for a horror protagonist — he has *reasons* to know maintenance tunnels, utility access, back routes.
- **The everyman, on purpose.** Average smarts — not dumb, just a guy who needed a job and this one paid well. He is the audience surrogate. Direct precedent: **Isaac Clarke (Dead Space)** — a blue-collar engineer with tools, not a soldier or a chosen one. We are telling *the janitor's* story, not the chosen one's. What pulls him deeper is **curiosity, not destiny.**
- **Arc question (open — see below):** Right now he's a survivor roped into action, armed initially with a metal pipe.

### The "chosen one" problem — SOLVED

We don't want Marcus special via a lazy immunity reveal. The fix is environmental, not biological:

- The breach happens **early and loud** — unmistakable. "What the fuck was that?"
- Marcus survives because he's **inside a protected/shielded room** at the moment of the breach (the research/testing room he was assigned to clean — see Opening Sequence). Pure luck of the schedule. He isn't chosen; he's just *where the protection was.*
- He's **completely unaware** it even happened — possibly with music blasting in his earbud, isolating him further. He walks out expecting a normal night.
- Some other people also survived. He's not unique — just lucky and present.
- **The story is about what he does with the second chance, not why he got it.** Specialness lives in his choices, not his biology.

> ✅ The protected room collapses two problems into one solution: it's *why he survives* AND a *clue* (someone built shielding here, which means the org knew there was something to contain). See Open Gap.

## Structure & Supporting Cast

POV discipline: **Marcus is the everyman, not the hero of the lore.** There's a bigger story happening — but we experience it sideways, through the janitor who keeps wandering into things he shouldn't.

- **The guide NPC** — whoever Marcus actually *meets.* Knows the containment situation and ropes Marcus in. This is the "voice guiding the everyman through the disaster" structure (Dead Space, BioShock). Can be a surviving scientist, a handler, security. The bigger story at the rift stays mostly off-screen and pieced-together — Marcus is a nobody to it, and that's the point.

> ⚠️ Guard rail: the guide can be morally grey, agenda-driven, even Polaris-complicit — but must **not** turn out to be the secret villain. That quietly resurrects the Atlas/mastermind twist we cut. Humans can be *culpable* without being the *antagonist.* The big-bad stays the void.

The campus's **tram system + many stops** is the structural spine for these encounters — each stop is a chance to meet a survivor, find a piece of the bigger story, or get pulled one step further into containment.

---

The biggest pivot. The villain is **no longer** a sophisticated, hidden mastermind (the BioShock-Atlas false-ally / "secret intelligence behind it all" archetype). It's the opposite:

- A **primordial, mindless infection.** No grand plan, no negotiation, no reveal that recontextualizes everything. Just *spread, command, conquer.*
- Sprawling and total. It takes over minds and bodies. It doesn't *want* anything in a human sense — it consumes.
- **What it actually is:** an incursion from *elsewhere.* The "breach" is **literal** — a contained break in dimensions (the rift logic of *Stranger Things* / *Gravity Falls* S2) that Polaris was holding shut. The void is what comes through when the seal fails. It's not a spontaneous infection; it's a door that opened.
- **If it has a voice at all,** the model is the **Dark Presence from Alan Wake 1** — an oppressive, atmospheric malevolence, not a chatty Bond villain.

The horror here is helplessness. You can't outwit it. You can only survive it and try to contain/escape it.

## Opening Sequence

Marcus is **new** — first day or first week. This does triple duty: natural tutorial logic (he's learning the building, so the player learns it too), low familiarity with coworkers, and a believable reason he's stuck with grunt work at 2 AM.

Beat by beat:

1. **The assignment.** Marcus is tasked with cleaning a specific research/testing room. It's protected/shielded — logs and signage establish this *early*, but framed so first-day Marcus doesn't grasp the implication (the player can piece it together later).
2. **The breach happens while he's inside.** He's saved by the shielding and **completely unaware** — likely music blasting in his earbud. (Parked idea: sync the breach moment to a beat in the music. Strong, save for later.)
3. **He steps out to keep cleaning.** Expecting normal. Instead, the coworker he *just spoke to* is now void-possessed. The personal gut-punch lands immediately — not a stranger, someone he had a conversation with minutes ago.
4. **The earbud starts screaming interference** the moment he leaves the shielded room. This is the in-fiction birth of the HSO mechanic — and it explains *why he can track it* without any contrivance.

### Why this works (the unification)

Consider making the void's mind-corruption **propagate as a signal**, with the room being **signal-shielded**. Then every piece pulls the same rope:
- The room saving him and the music drowning it out become the *same* protection, not two ideas.
- The same earbud that isolated him from the corruption now detects its frequency as interference.
- The breach-synced-to-music beat becomes thematically load-bearing, not just a cool moment.
- The title *Signal* means survival, detection, threat, and corruption-mechanism are all one concept.

Optional — take it or leave it — but it's the tightest version.

## Tone & References

The target blend:

- **Dead Space** — body horror, necromorph-style corrupted enemies, isolation, the campus-as-derelict-ship feeling.
- **Silent Hill 2** — psychological, oppressive dread over cheap jump scares; the sense that the place itself is wrong. Crucially, SH2's radio-static principle (sound as the player's early-warning sense) is the direct ancestor of Signal's **HSO audio interference tracker** (see below).
- **Alan Wake 1** (specifically *1*, not 2) — atmospheric primordial dread, the Dark Presence energy. *1* is the scope target; *2*'s ambition is acknowledged but out of reach for a solo dev, and that's a deliberate, healthy constraint.

Tonal guardrail: AW1 had moments of cheese/corn that the game *earned back* over time. We want the atmospheric dread without leaning into the corny. Sincere, grounded horror.

### HSO audio interference tracker

**The HSO is diegetic: it's Marcus's bluetooth earbud.** No sci-fi gadget — the void's presence registers as interference in the earbud he already had in while cleaning. The radio-static principle without a radio. As the void gets closer, the interference rises; sound becomes the player's primary threat-detection sense.

Design notes:
- **Proximity, not binary.** Interference should scale with distance/density (SH static), so it stays useful even once the void is everywhere — it reads as *how close*, not *present/absent*.
- **Title payoff.** A game called *Signal*, where survival, detection, and the threat all run through literal signal interference. The thing you track and the thing hunting you are the same frequency.
- **Open mechanical lever:** the earbud is a single point of detection. Losing it, removing it, or battery as a resource are all available tension knobs — park for later.

## Enemies

A conscious revision from the original "only sprawling, non-humanoid figures" rule:

- **Humanoid void-corrupted (fodder):** Necromorph-style. The former coworkers, residents, scientists — now hollowed out. These give you close, personal, *recognizable* horror. The "that used to be a person" gut-punch.
- **Non-humanoid void spawn / monsters:** The sprawling, alien creature threats. The stuff that's clearly *not* from here.

Having both gives you tonal range: the tragic-personal (humanoids) and the alien-unknowable (spawn).

## Locations / Progression

Marcus fights outward and upward through the campus's systems:

1. **(Start)** Wherever the night shift puts him — janitorial, low and unglamorous.
2. **Water treatment facility** — utilities, industrial, claustrophobic.
3. **Office spaces** — the human, lived-in administrative layer.
4. **Tram system** — connective tissue between campus zones; transit set-pieces.
5. **Research labs** — the epicenter. Likely where the *why* lives.

Natural difficulty and revelation curve: you start in the basement of the world (literally and socially) and climb toward the source.

## THE OPEN GAP — where the next discussion starts

Origin is now largely locked: **Polaris was containing an extra-dimensional rift; the breach is that containment failing; the void is what comes through.** The open questions have moved downstream:

> **Who ropes Marcus in, and what does re-sealing actually require?**

- **The guide NPC.** Who pulls Marcus into containment, and what's their stake? (Grey is fine; secret-mastermind is not.)
- **Containment objective.** What does re-sealing the rift actually require — and why does it fall to Marcus? Keep it circumstance, not destiny.
- **What Marcus learns** as curiosity drags him off the escape route and into Polaris's secrets.
- **The ending fork:** escape / sacrifice / containment — which, and what does it cost him?

## Constraints (reality check)

- **Solo dev.** Scope discipline is a feature, not a limitation. AW1 over AW2 is the right instinct.
- **UE5, 2026 aesthetic.** Curated asset pipeline — quality weapons, animations that read as professional, not "a kid made this."
- Production value AND story quality are both non-negotiable goals.
